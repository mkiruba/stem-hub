# Competitions

A deadline and an audience change everything. Learners who drift on open-ended projects often transform when there's a date, a rulebook and other teams.

UK-focused, but most of these run internationally.

---

## Robotics

### [Pi Wars](https://piwars.org/) — ages 11+ and adults
Raspberry Pi robots competing in non-destructive challenges, both autonomous and remote-controlled. Held over a weekend in Cambridge, with school, family, hobbyist and solo categories. Open to entrants worldwide. The [YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/c/piwars) has years of past runs, and competitor blogs are an excellent free build-log resource.

**Why it's good:** the challenge variety rewards different strengths, the community is unusually welcoming, and the barrier to a competitive entry is low. Probably the best first competition for a UK Pi-based team.

### [FIRST LEGO League](https://firstlegoleague.org/) — ages 9–16
⚠️ **The 2026–2027 BIOGLOW season is the final one.** LEGO Education has not renewed its partnership with FIRST. Two editions run in parallel: *Founders Edition* on SPIKE hardware (final season 2027–2028) and *Future Edition* on the new Computer Science & AI kits. LEGO Education plans its own separate competition from 2027–2028.

[Season materials](https://firstlegoleague.org/season) — rubrics, scoresheets, judging flowcharts and twelve support sessions — are free to download and useful as a structure even outside the competition.

### [VEX Robotics Competition](https://www.vexrobotics.com/competition) — ages 8–18+
VEX IQ (primary/lower secondary) and VEX V5 (upper secondary). Metal construction system, well-established, and now the strongest continuing option for schools leaving FLL. Runs through [RoboticsEducation.org](https://www.roboticseducation.org/).

### [FIRST Tech Challenge](https://www.firstinspires.org/robotics/ftc) / [FIRST Robotics Competition](https://www.firstinspires.org/robotics/frc) — ages 12–18
Where FLL teams are now pointed. FRC in particular is a serious commitment — significant budget, a six-week build season, and a large mentor team — but the payoff is correspondingly large.

### [RoboCup Junior](https://junior.robocup.org/) — ages up to 19
Soccer, rescue and OnStage leagues. Platform-agnostic, so a Pico or Arduino robot is entirely viable. Strong international pathway.

### [World Robot Olympiad](https://wro-association.org/) — ages 8–19
Genuinely platform-agnostic, which is rare — RoboMission accepts any hardware (LEGO, Pico, Arduino, custom), Future Engineers is an autonomous-vehicle challenge, and Future Innovators is an open, self-defined project judged like a science fair. A UK national final feeds the international finals. Worth serious consideration as the default robotics competition here, precisely because it doesn't lock a team into one hardware platform the way FLL, VEX or FTC do.

### [MATE ROV Competition](https://materovcompetition.org/) — ages 11–19+ (school divisions; Explorer class extends to university)
Underwater robotics: design and build a remotely-operated vehicle to complete missions in a pool. The entry-level build is a PVC-pipe frame, a bilge-pump motor for thrust, and a tether — well under £60 in parts, and a genuinely new theme for this repo (nothing else here touches marine engineering). International, with regional qualifiers.

## Engineering & design

### [Tomorrow's Engineers EEP Robotics Challenge](https://www.tomorrowsengineers.org.uk/) — UK, ages 11–14
Free to enter for UK schools, LEGO-based, and designed to need no prior robotics experience from the teacher. A good first step for a school with no existing programme.

### [F1 in Schools](https://www.f1inschools.co.uk/) — ages 9–19
CAD, CFD, manufacturing, and a business case alongside the engineering. Broader than pure coding, and reaches learners who want the design and communication side.

### [Formula Student](https://www.imeche.org/events/formula-student) — university level
Where this pathway ends up. Worth showing to 16-year-olds as a destination.

### [Green Power Education Trust](https://www.greenpower.co.uk/) — UK, ages 9–25
Design, build and race a genuinely electric single-seater car — Goblin class for the youngest, up through Formula 24 and 24+. More mechanical and electrical engineering than pure coding, and a strong option for learners who want to build something that physically races other teams' cars.

## Recognition for your own project

Everything above is a competition with a fixed format. These aren't — they badge or showcase a self-directed project of the learner's own choosing, which fits this repository's default mode of working better than any league does.

### [CREST Awards](https://www.crestawards.org/) — UK, ages 5–19, free
The British Science Association's project-based award scheme: Discovery, Bronze, Silver and Gold, roughly matched to the [age bands](../docs/02-age-bands-and-tracks.md) used throughout this site. Any project from any platform page here qualifies — there's no required format, hardware or topic. If a learner has already built something from this repo and wants recognition for it rather than a new competition to enter, this is the answer.

### [The Big Bang Competition](https://www.thebigbangfair.co.uk/big-bang-competition/) — UK, ages 11–18, free
EngineeringUK's national science and engineering fair pathway, judged categories across all STEM subjects, culminating in The Big Bang Fair. The closest thing on this page to a general-purpose science fair, which nothing else here provides — every other entry is robotics-, coding- or space-specific.

## Space

### [Astro Pi](https://astro-pi.org/) — ages up to 19, free
Write Python that runs on Raspberry Pi hardware aboard the ISS. Two tiers: **Mission Zero** takes a single lesson and every valid entry runs in space, which makes it the single highest-impact-per-hour activity in this entire repository. **Mission Space Lab** is a serious multi-month research project.

Run by the Raspberry Pi Foundation with ESA. Free. If you do one thing from this page, do Mission Zero.

### [ESA CanSat](https://www.esa.int/Education/CanSat) — ages 14–19
Build a satellite the size of a drinks can, launch it to ~1 km, recover and analyse the telemetry. National rounds including a UK competition, then a European final.

### [UK Rocketry Challenge](https://www.ukrocketry.co.uk/) — ages 11–18
Design, build and fly a rocket to a target altitude with a fragile payload.

## Coding (no hardware)

- **[Advent of Code](https://adventofcode.com/)** — December daily puzzles, free, addictive, all languages
- **[Bebras](https://www.bebras.uk/)** — UK computational thinking challenge, ages 6–18, free
- **[UK Bebras → Oxford Computing Challenge](https://www.olympiad.org.uk/)** → **British Informatics Olympiad** — the competitive programming pathway
- **[Coolest Projects](https://coolestprojects.org/)** — Raspberry Pi Foundation showcase rather than a competition. No winners, no losers, any project accepted. Excellent for learners who freeze under competitive pressure.
- **[Technovation Girls](https://technovationgirls.org/)** — ages 8–18, free, international with UK teams. All-girls teams build a mobile app addressing a real community problem, then pitch it like a startup — coding plus entrepreneurship, and a direct answer to the gender-balance point raised in the [wearables theme](../themes/README.md#wearables-e-textiles).
- **[NCSC CyberFirst](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/cyberfirst/overview)** — UK, ages 11–17, free. The National Cyber Security Centre's free courses, bursaries and the CyberFirst Girls Competition (Year 8). No hardware, no cost — and cybersecurity isn't covered anywhere else in this repository.

## Choosing one

| If your group is… | Try |
|---|---|
| A school with no existing programme, no budget | Astro Pi Mission Zero, then Bebras |
| A school with LEGO already | FLL Founders Edition while it lasts, then VEX IQ |
| A hobbyist family or small club | Pi Wars |
| Ambitious 16–18s wanting a personal statement | CanSat, Astro Pi Mission Space Lab, FRC |
| Learners who dislike competition | Coolest Projects |
| Already finished a project, want it recognised | CREST Awards |
| Want a robotics league that isn't locked to one hardware platform | World Robot Olympiad |
| Interested in something other than wheeled robots | MATE ROV (underwater), Green Power (electric cars) |

## Running a team: what actually helps

- **Start earlier than feels necessary.** Every experienced coach says this and every new coach ignores it.
- **Build two of everything critical.** Robots break the week before. Spare motors and a spare controller cost less than a season.
- **Log everything.** Photos, decisions, failed attempts. Most competitions score the engineering journal or judged presentation heavily, and it's the part teams leave until last. It's also the part that most resembles real engineering.
- **Let them lose properly.** The learning is in the debrief. Coaches who build the robot produce teams who learned nothing and know it.
- **Watch past runs on YouTube.** Pi Wars and FLL both have extensive archives. An hour of watching saves a month of guessing.
