# How to use this collection

## For a parent

Start with [the buying guide](03-buying-guide-uk.md), pick Bundle A or B depending on age, then work through the "Start with" list in the matching [track](02-age-bands-and-tracks.md). Resist the urge to buy the exciting expensive thing first.

Your actual job during the first few months is mostly to sit nearby, resist solving the problem, and ask "what did you expect to happen?" when something doesn't work. That question does more than any tutorial.

## For a teacher or club leader

1. [Free curricula](../resources/free-curricula.md) has complete schemes of work — start there rather than assembling lessons from individual projects.
2. Standardise on one platform. See [buying guide § clubs](03-buying-guide-uk.md#buying-for-a-club-or-school).
3. Read [safety](04-safety.md) and adapt your risk assessment before the first hardware session.
4. Use [themes](../themes/README.md) to find projects that fit a topic you're already teaching — a data-logging project drops neatly into a science scheme, a MIDI controller into music tech.

## For a learner working alone

Pick a [theme](../themes/README.md) you actually care about rather than a platform. Then find the cheapest board on that theme's page that does what you want, and follow one tutorial series to completion before jumping to another.

The most common failure mode is starting six projects and finishing none. Finish one bad project rather than starting three good ones.

## Reading the entries

```
> **[Resource name](url)** — `Difficulty` `Type`
> What it teaches, in one line. Parts: what you need to own. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.
```

**Difficulty:** `Foundation` (≈11–13) · `Intermediate` (≈14–16) · `Advanced` (≈17+)

**Type:** `Video` · `Tutorial` · `Blog` · `GitHub` · `Curriculum` · `Simulator` · `Reference`

Where a resource spans levels, the tag shows where it *starts*.

## What qualifies for inclusion

Every entry must be:

1. **Free to access** — no paywall, no mandatory account, no trial that expires.
2. **Buildable** — maps to components stocked by a listed supplier, or runs in a free simulator.
3. **Currently live** — checked automatically each week.
4. **Age-appropriate** — suitable for the stated band, including language and safety framing.

Resources that are merely *good* but paid (Arduino's official courses, most Udemy content, CS50x certificates) are deliberately excluded. There's more than enough free material to fill a decade.
