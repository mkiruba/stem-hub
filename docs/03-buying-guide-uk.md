# Buying guide (UK)

What to buy, from where, and in what order. Written for parents, teachers and club leaders who want to spend once and spend well.

---

## The four suppliers, and what each is actually good for

### [The Pi Hut](https://thepihut.com/)
UK-based, next-day delivery, no import faff. The default choice for anything Raspberry Pi, Pico, micro:bit or Pimoroni. They stock Adafruit lines too, usually cheaper than importing directly once you account for duty. Their [tutorial blog](https://thepihut.com/blogs/raspberry-pi-tutorials) is free and well-written. **Start here for most orders.**

### [RS Components](https://uk.rs-online.com/web/)
Industrial supplier, so: enormous catalogue, proper datasheets, trade accounts, and reliable bulk pricing. Best for discrete components (resistor kits, capacitors, connectors), tools, and anything you need a hundred of. They're also an official Raspberry Pi distributor. Less hobbyist-friendly browsing — you need to know roughly what you want. **Best for schools with purchase orders, and for component restocking.**

### [Adafruit](https://www.adafruit.com/)
US-based. The hardware is excellent and often unique (Circuit Playground, Feather, NeoPixels, STEMMA QT sensors), but the real value is the [Learning System](https://learn.adafruit.com/) — thousands of free, well-produced guides, most under a Creative Commons licence. Shipping to the UK costs and takes time; check The Pi Hut or Pimoroni first for the same part. **Buy the learning content for free, buy the hardware locally where you can.**

### [LEGO Education](https://education.lego.com/)
See [the LEGO transition page](05-lego-2026-transition.md) before spending anything here. SPIKE sales ended June 2026.

Worth adding: **[Pimoroni](https://shop.pimoroni.com/)** (Sheffield) and **[Kitronik](https://kitronik.co.uk/)** (Nottingham). Both UK, both excellent, both publish free tutorials. Kitronik in particular writes classroom-ready micro:bit lesson material.

## Recommended starter bundles

### Bundle A — Foundation, one learner (~£45)

| Item | Approx | Why |
|---|---|---|
| BBC micro:bit v2 | £15 | Accelerometer, mic, speaker, radio, all onboard |
| Crocodile clip leads (10) | £4 | No soldering required |
| Kitronik or Pi Hut micro:bit starter kit | £20 | LEDs, buzzer, servo, sensors |
| Battery pack + AAA | £5 | Untethered projects matter more than you'd think |

Gets you through roughly thirty projects before you need anything else.

### Bundle B — Intermediate, one learner (~£70)

| Item | Approx | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Raspberry Pi Pico 2 W (with headers) | £8 | Dual-core, WiFi, MicroPython or C |
| Half-size breadboard ×2 | £6 | One for the current project, one for the next |
| Jumper wire assortment | £6 | Buy more than you think |
| Component starter kit (resistors, LEDs, buttons, transistors) | £18 | RS is good value here |
| Multimeter | £15 | Non-negotiable. Cheap is fine. |
| Soldering iron (temperature-controlled) | £25 | See [safety](04-safety.md) |
| Assorted I²C sensors (STEMMA QT / Qwiic) | £15 | Plug-in sensors, no wiring errors |

### Bundle C — Advanced, one learner (~£160)

Raspberry Pi 5 (4GB) + official PSU + microSD + Camera Module 3 + motor driver HAT + chassis and motors. Add a 3D printer if the club has one; add KiCad and a cheap PCB fab run when they're ready.

### Bundle D — Classroom of 30 (~£700)

15× micro:bit v2 (pairs work better than solo), 15× accessory kits, a class set of crocodile leads, one teacher demo Raspberry Pi, and a consumables budget. Buy through RS on a PO. Budget 15% annually for replacement — things break, and that's fine.

## Buy-later, not buy-now

Common overspends, in rough order of regret:

- **Big robot kits before basics.** A £200 kit that assembles in one afternoon and then sits on a shelf teaches less than £40 of loose parts.
- **A 3D printer, early.** Useful eventually. A distraction at the start — you'll spend the term debugging the printer, not the robot.
- **Premium soldering stations.** A £25 temperature-controlled iron is genuinely fine for years.
- **Discontinued LEGO at collector prices.** See above.
- **Oscilloscopes.** Real, but not before advanced track.

---

## Buy-first, no regrets

- **A multimeter.** Cheapest item on this list, highest diagnostic value.
- **More jumper wires than you need.** They vanish.
- **A second board of whatever you're using.** Halves debugging time — you can swap to isolate the fault.
- **Storage.** A compartment box. Loose components in a drawer become no components.
- **Good side cutters and wire strippers.** Bad ones make everything harder and are how people get hurt.

## Buying for a club or school

- **Standardise.** One platform, one version, across the whole group. Mixed hardware multiplies your support burden by more than you'd expect.
- **Pairs, not solos.** Two learners per kit produces more discussion, more debugging and less passive following of instructions.
- **Consumables budget.** Wires, batteries, solder, LEDs. Roughly 15% of hardware spend per year.
- **Check for grants.** [Raspberry Pi Foundation](https://www.raspberrypi.org/), [IET](https://education.theiet.org/), [Tomorrow's Engineers](https://www.tomorrowsengineers.org.uk/) and STEM Learning all run UK funding routes. [Code Club](https://codeclub.org/) provides free resources and training.
- **Simulate first.** [Wokwi](https://wokwi.com/) and [Tinkercad Circuits](https://www.tinkercad.com/circuits) are free and let you validate a project plan before committing budget.
