# Video channels

Free YouTube channels worth following, with the good starting point flagged for each. All active as of August 2026.

---

## Structured courses (start here for self-directed learners)

**[Paul McWhorter](https://www.youtube.com/@paulmcwhorter)** — `Intermediate`
Numbered lesson series with homework at the end of each video, for Arduino, Raspberry Pi and Python. This is the closest thing on YouTube to an actual course, and it's the best free option for a motivated teenager working alone. Start with lesson 1 of the Arduino series and don't skip.

**[Kevin McAleer / kevsrobots](https://www.youtube.com/@kevinmcaleer28)** — `Intermediate`
Pico robotics, prolific and UK-based. Everything is open-sourced, and [kevsrobots.com](https://www.kevsrobots.com/) has free written courses alongside. Best single channel for Pico-based robots.

**[Core Electronics](https://www.youtube.com/@Core-Electronics)** — `Foundation` `Intermediate`
Clear, well-produced, spanning micro:bit, Pico and Pi. Strong on explaining *why*.

## Electronics theory

**[Ben Eater](https://www.youtube.com/@BenEater)** — `Advanced`
Builds an 8-bit computer from logic gates on breadboards, then a 6502 machine, explaining every decision. Also networking from first principles. There is genuinely nothing comparable at any price. Free written material at [eater.net](https://eater.net/). Start with the [8-bit computer playlist](https://eater.net/8bit).

**[GreatScott!](https://www.youtube.com/@greatscottlab)** — `Intermediate`
Short, dense component explainers. Excellent on transistors, MOSFETs and power supplies.

**[Afrotechmods](https://www.youtube.com/@Afrotechmods)** — `Intermediate`
No-nonsense component tutorials, short and clear.

**[EEVblog](https://www.youtube.com/@EEVblog)** — `Advanced`
Dave Jones on test gear, teardowns and design practice. His multimeter tutorials are the standard reference.

**[ElectroBOOM](https://www.youtube.com/@ElectroBOOM)** — `Intermediate`
Real theory delivered through deliberately painful demonstrations. Memorable and effective. **Supervisor note:** he intentionally does dangerous things with mains for comedy. He flags this; younger viewers may not register it. Worth an explicit conversation — see [safety](../docs/04-safety.md).

## Robotics & mechanical

**[James Bruton / XRobots](https://www.youtube.com/@jamesbruton)** — `Advanced`
Self-balancing robots, quadrupeds, mecanum drives, exoskeletons. Everything open-sourced on [GitHub](https://github.com/XRobots). Excellent at showing the failures alongside the successes, which is rarer than it should be.

**[DroneBot Workshop](https://www.youtube.com/@Dronebotworkshop)** — `Intermediate` `Advanced`
Long-form and thorough, particularly on motors, drivers and sensors — the theory most robot tutorials skip. Written companions at [dronebotworkshop.com](https://dronebotworkshop.com/).

**[Jeremy Fielding](https://www.youtube.com/@JeremyFieldingSr)** — `Advanced`
Mechanical engineering, motors, actuators, gearing. The physical-side knowledge that pure electronics channels don't cover.

**[Anton's Mindstorms](https://www.youtube.com/@antonsmindstorms)** — `Intermediate`
LEGO robotics done properly, in Python via Pybricks.

## Raspberry Pi & Linux

**[ExplainingComputers](https://www.youtube.com/@ExplainingComputers)** — `Intermediate`
Christopher Barnatt. Calm, precise, unusually good at explaining what a board is actually for.

**[Jeff Geerling](https://www.youtube.com/@JeffGeerling)** — `Advanced`
Pi hardware deep dives, clustering, networking, storage. For learners past the beginner stage.

**[Raspberry Pi Foundation](https://www.youtube.com/@RaspberryPiFoundation)** — `Foundation`
Official educational content and teacher CPD.

## Making & inspiration

**[Adafruit](https://www.youtube.com/@adafruit)** — `Intermediate`
*John Park's Workshop* and *Ask an Engineer*.

**[Becky Stern](https://www.youtube.com/@beckystern)** — `Intermediate`
Wearables and soft circuits. A genuinely different entry point that reaches learners robots don't.

**[Bitluni](https://www.youtube.com/@bitluni)** — `Advanced`
Making microcontrollers do things they shouldn't manage — VGA, audio, unexpected peripherals.

**[Andreas Spiess](https://www.youtube.com/@AndreasSpiess)** — `Advanced`
ESP32, IoT, LoRa. Rigorous — he measures rather than assumes, which models good engineering practice.

**[Estefannie](https://www.youtube.com/@estefanniegg)** — `Intermediate`
Playful, well-produced project builds. Good for learners who need the fun framing first.

**[Simone Giertz](https://www.youtube.com/@simonegiertz)** — `Intermediate`
Machines that don't quite work, made joyfully. The best available antidote to perfectionism, which stops more young makers than any technical barrier. Occasional strong language — preview before showing to a class.

## Using video well

Video is the worst medium for reference and one of the best for motivation. The pattern that works:

1. **Watch** a project video to decide you want to build it.
2. **Switch to written** instructions to actually build it — you can't scrub a video with solder in your hand.
3. **Return to video** for the bits where seeing the physical technique matters (soldering, mechanical assembly).

Channels that publish written companions — DroneBot Workshop, Kevin McAleer, Ben Eater — are worth more than their view counts suggest for exactly this reason.
