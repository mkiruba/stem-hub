    Video channels

    Free YouTube channels worth following, with the good starting point flagged for each. All active as of August 2026.

    Structured courses (start here for self-directed learners)

    Paul McWhorter — Intermediate Numbered lesson series with homework at the end of each video, for Arduino, Raspberry Pi and Python. This is the closest thing on YouTube to an actual course, and it's the best free option for a motivated teenager working alone. Start with lesson 1 of the Arduino series and don't skip. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.

    Kevin McAleer / kevsrobots — Intermediate Pico robotics, prolific and UK-based. Everything is open-sourced, and kevsrobots.com has free written courses alongside. Best single channel for Pico-based robots. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.

    Core Electronics — Foundation Intermediate Clear, well-produced, spanning micro:bit, Pico and Pi. Strong on explaining why. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.

    Electronics theory

    Ben Eater — Advanced Builds an 8-bit computer from logic gates on breadboards, then a 6502 machine, explaining every decision. Also networking from first principles. There is genuinely nothing comparable at any price. Free written material at eater.net. Start with the 8-bit computer playlist. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.

    GreatScott! — Intermediate Short, dense component explainers. Excellent on transistors, MOSFETs and power supplies. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.

    Afrotechmods — Intermediate No-nonsense component tutorials, short and clear. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.

    EEVblog — Advanced Dave Jones on test gear, teardowns and design practice. His multimeter tutorials are the standard reference. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.

    ElectroBOOM — Intermediate Real theory delivered through deliberately painful demonstrations. Memorable and effective. Supervisor note: he intentionally does dangerous things with mains for comedy. He flags this; younger viewers may not register it. Worth an explicit conversation — see safety. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.

    Robotics & mechanical

    James Bruton / XRobots — Advanced Self-balancing robots, quadrupeds, mecanum drives, exoskeletons. Everything open-sourced on GitHub. Excellent at showing the failures alongside the successes, which is rarer than it should be. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.

    DroneBot Workshop — Intermediate Advanced Long-form and thorough, particularly on motors, drivers and sensors — the theory most robot tutorials skip. Written companions at dronebotworkshop.com. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.

    Jeremy Fielding — Advanced Mechanical engineering, motors, actuators, gearing. The physical-side knowledge that pure electronics channels don't cover. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.

    Anton's Mindstorms — Intermediate LEGO robotics done properly, in Python via Pybricks. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.

    Raspberry Pi & Linux

    ExplainingComputers — Intermediate Christopher Barnatt. Calm, precise, unusually good at explaining what a board is actually for. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.

    Jeff Geerling — Advanced Pi hardware deep dives, clustering, networking, storage. For learners past the beginner stage. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.

    Raspberry Pi Foundation — Foundation Official educational content and teacher CPD. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.

    Making & inspiration

    Adafruit — Intermediate John Park's Workshop and Ask an Engineer. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.

    Becky Stern — Intermediate Wearables and soft circuits. A genuinely different entry point that reaches learners robots don't. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.

    Bitluni — Advanced Making microcontrollers do things they shouldn't manage — VGA, audio, unexpected peripherals. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.

    Andreas Spiess — Advanced ESP32, IoT, LoRa. Rigorous — he measures rather than assumes, which models good engineering practice. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.

    Estefannie — Intermediate Playful, well-produced project builds. Good for learners who need the fun framing first. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.

    Simone Giertz — Intermediate Machines that don't quite work, made joyfully. The best available antidote to perfectionism, which stops more young makers than any technical barrier. Occasional strong language — preview before showing to a class. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.

    Using video well

    Video is the worst medium for reference and one of the best for motivation. The pattern that works:

    1. Watch a project video to decide you want to build it.
    2. Switch to written instructions to actually build it — you can't scrub a video with solder in your hand.
    3. Return to video for the bits where seeing the physical technique matters (soldering, mechanical assembly).

    Channels that publish written companions — DroneBot Workshop, Kevin McAleer, Ben Eater — are worth more than their view counts suggest for exactly this reason.
