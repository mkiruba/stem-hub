    Free curricula

    Complete schemes of work, for teachers and club leaders who need sequenced material rather than individual projects. All free.

    UK curriculum-aligned

    Teach Computing (Raspberry Pi Foundation / NCCE) — Foundation Intermediate Advanced A complete Key Stage 1–4 computing curriculum: lesson plans, slides, worksheets, assessments, the lot. Mapped to the English National Curriculum, free to download, and genuinely usable off the shelf. If you teach computing in England, start here rather than anywhere else. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.

    Also offers free CPD courses for teachers.

    Isaac Computer Science — Advanced Free A-level computer science platform with topic material, self-marking questions and teacher tools. Built by the Raspberry Pi Foundation and the University of Cambridge. Excellent for 16–18. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.

    STEM Learning — Curriculum Large free resource library plus subsidised CPD. Also the route into STEM Ambassadors, who will come and speak to your group free of charge. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.

    Kitronik lesson plans — Foundation UK, classroom-tested, matched to affordable kit. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.

    Platform curricula

    micro:bit teaching resources — Foundation Free schemes of work and a full introductory computing course, mapped to curricula in several countries. Includes micro:bit Classroom, a live tool letting a teacher see every learner's code in real time with no accounts required. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.

    Code Club projects — Foundation Free, sequenced, club-ready. Code Club itself provides free training and support for volunteers starting a club. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.

    CoderDojo — Foundation Intermediate Free community coding clubs with published materials — the "Sushi Cards" are well-designed self-paced worksheets. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.

    Microsoft MakeCode courses — Foundation Curriculum Structured multi-week courses using blocks, with an easy path into Python. Parts needed: micro:bit.

    Get Started with Raspberry Pi Pico — Intermediate Curriculum Official multi-part course introducing MicroPython programming, breadboard circuitry, and sensor interfaces on the RP2040 and RP2350. Parts needed: Raspberry Pi Pico, breadboard, LEDs, resistors.

    Adafruit CircuitPython 101 — Intermediate Curriculum The fastest path to physical Python: drag-and-drop code, USB storage workflow, and interfacing hundreds of plug-and-play STEMMA QT/Qwiic sensors. Parts needed: Any CircuitPython-compatible microcontroller board.

    PrimeLessons — Foundation Intermediate Curriculum Structured lessons for SPIKE Prime covering motors, sensors, line following, and Python programming by Droid Robotics. Parts needed: SPIKE Prime set.

    LEGO Education lesson plans — Foundation Curriculum Free and curriculum-mapped, still hosted for retired kits. See the LEGO transition note. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.

    FIRST LEGO League season materials — Curriculum Twelve sessions of support slides, rubrics and scoresheets — free, and a useful structure even outside the competition. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.

    Electronics & embedded systems courses

    Paul McWhorter’s New Arduino Series — Intermediate Video The gold-standard 68-lesson structured electronics course with assignments: digital/analog I/O, Ohm’s law, pull-up resistors, shift registers, and C++. Parts needed: Arduino Uno or Nano, breadboard, basic electronics kit.

    Lessons in Electric Circuits (All About Circuits) — Intermediate Advanced Tutorial Rigorous 6-volume open-source textbook and course covering DC/AC analysis, semiconductors, digital logic, and schematic reading. Parts needed: Multimeter, breadboard, discrete components.

    Embedded Systems: Shape the World (UT Austin / Valvano) — Advanced Curriculum University-level introduction to embedded systems and low-level C programming on ARM Cortex-M microcontrollers. Free textbook, labs, and simulator. Parts needed: ARM Cortex-M board or free software simulator.

    Computer science & advanced robotics

    CS50P: Introduction to Programming with Python (Harvard) — Intermediate Advanced Curriculum Comprehensive introduction to programming in Python covering functions, loops, exceptions, testing, object-oriented programming, and libraries. 100% free to audit. Parts needed: Computer with internet access.

    CS50x: Introduction to Computer Science (Harvard) — Advanced Curriculum Free to audit — all lectures, problem sets and materials covering algorithms, data structures, memory, and web development. Excellent for 17+. Parts needed: Computer with internet access.

    Ben Eater’s 8-Bit Computer — Advanced Video Build a fully working 8-bit computer on breadboards from basic TTL logic gates, learning computer architecture, ALU design, and machine code. Parts needed: Breadboards, 7400-series logic chips, EEPROM.

    Introduction to TinyML (Harvard / tinyml.seas.harvard.edu) — Advanced Curriculum Free educational resources and lectures on deploying machine learning models to ultra-low-power microcontrollers. Parts needed: Arduino Nano 33 BLE or Raspberry Pi Pico.

    ROS 2 Official Tutorials — Advanced Tutorial Progressive guide from the Open Source Robotics Foundation on nodes, topics, services, actions, and creating full autonomous robot software stacks. Parts needed: Linux computer or Raspberry Pi 4/5.

    ESA CanSat Training Academy — Intermediate Advanced Curriculum European Space Agency guides for designing, coding, and launching a soda-can-sized satellite payload with atmospheric telemetry. Parts needed: Microcontroller, sensors, radio transceiver.

    nand2tetris — Advanced Curriculum Build a computer from NAND gates up through a CPU, assembler, compiler and operating system. Free course materials; the first half runs entirely in simulation. Parts needed: Computer with internet access.

    Khan Academy — Computing — Foundation Intermediate Curriculum Free, structured, with automatic exercises. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.

    Carnegie Mellon Robotics Academy — Intermediate Curriculum Robotics curricula with a meaningful free tier and strong computational-thinking framing. Parts needed: See tutorial for details.

    Assembling your own scheme of work

    If none of the above quite fits, the pattern that works over a school year:

    Term 1 — Foundation, no soldering. micro:bit, block coding moving to Python via the MakeCode toggle. One finished project per fortnight. The goal is confidence and the habit of finishing.

    Term 2 — Breadboarding and sensors. Move to Pico or keep micro:bit with a breakout. Introduce the multimeter, Ohm's law, pull-up resistors. Projects span two to three sessions. Introduce Git here, on something small.

    Term 3 — An open project. Learners choose. They will pick something too ambitious; let them, then help them scope it down in week two rather than week eight. End with a showcase — an audience does more for quality than any amount of marking.

    Three principles that matter more than the content:

    • Finish things. A completed simple project beats an abandoned impressive one, every time. This is the single biggest predictor of whether a learner continues.
    • Pairs, not solos. Two per kit produces discussion, debugging and explanation. One per kit produces silent instruction-following.
    • Let the failures happen. A circuit that doesn't work is the lesson. Rescuing too early removes the entire learning opportunity — and learners can tell when they've been rescued.
