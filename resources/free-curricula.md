# Free curricula

Complete schemes of work, for teachers and club leaders who need sequenced material rather than individual projects. All free.

---

## UK curriculum-aligned

**[Teach Computing (Raspberry Pi Foundation / NCCE)](https://teachcomputing.org/curriculum)** — `Foundation` `Intermediate` `Advanced`
A complete Key Stage 1–4 computing curriculum: lesson plans, slides, worksheets, assessments, the lot. Mapped to the English National Curriculum, free to download, and genuinely usable off the shelf. If you teach computing in England, start here rather than anywhere else.

Also offers free CPD courses for teachers.

**[Isaac Computer Science](https://isaaccomputerscience.org/)** — `Advanced`
Free A-level computer science platform with topic material, self-marking questions and teacher tools. Built by the Raspberry Pi Foundation and the University of Cambridge. Excellent for 16–18.

**[STEM Learning](https://www.stem.org.uk/)** — `Curriculum`
Large free resource library plus subsidised CPD. Also the route into STEM Ambassadors, who will come and speak to your group free of charge.

**[Kitronik lesson plans](https://kitronik.co.uk/blogs/resources)** — `Foundation`
UK, classroom-tested, matched to affordable kit.

## Platform curricula

**[micro:bit teaching resources](https://microbit.org/teach/)** — `Foundation`
Free schemes of work and a full introductory computing course, mapped to curricula in several countries. Includes [micro:bit Classroom](https://classroom.microbit.org/), a live tool letting a teacher see every learner's code in real time with no accounts required.

**[Code Club projects](https://projects.raspberrypi.org/en/codeclub)** — `Foundation`
Free, sequenced, club-ready. [Code Club](https://codeclub.org/) itself provides free training and support for volunteers starting a club.

**[CoderDojo](https://coderdojo.com/)** — `Foundation` `Intermediate`
Free community coding clubs with published materials — the "Sushi Cards" are well-designed self-paced worksheets.

**[Microsoft MakeCode courses](https://makecode.microbit.org/courses)** — `Foundation`
Structured multi-week courses using blocks, with an easy path into Python.

**[LEGO Education lesson plans](https://education.lego.com/en-gb/lessons/)** — `Foundation`
Free and curriculum-mapped, still hosted for retired kits. See [the LEGO transition note](../docs/05-lego-2026-transition.md).

**[FIRST LEGO League season materials](https://firstlegoleague.org/season)** — `Curriculum`
Twelve sessions of support slides, rubrics and scoresheets — free, and a useful structure even outside the competition.

## Computer science fundamentals

**[nand2tetris](https://www.nand2tetris.org/)** — `Advanced`
Build a computer from NAND gates up through a CPU, assembler, compiler and operating system. Free course materials; the first half runs entirely in simulation, so no hardware is needed. Demanding and outstanding.

**[CS50x (Harvard)](https://cs50.harvard.edu/x/)** — `Advanced`
Free to audit — all lectures, problem sets and materials. Only the certificate costs money. Excellent for 17+.

**[Khan Academy — Computing](https://www.khanacademy.org/computing)** — `Foundation` `Intermediate`
Free, structured, with automatic exercises.

**[Carnegie Mellon Robotics Academy](https://www.cmu.edu/roboticsacademy/)** — `Intermediate`
Robotics curricula, with a meaningful free tier. Their computational-thinking framing is unusually well thought through.

## Assembling your own scheme of work

If none of the above quite fits, the pattern that works over a school year:

**Term 1 — Foundation, no soldering.** micro:bit, block coding moving to Python via the MakeCode toggle. One finished project per fortnight. The goal is confidence and the habit of finishing.

**Term 2 — Breadboarding and sensors.** Move to Pico or keep micro:bit with a breakout. Introduce the multimeter, Ohm's law, pull-up resistors. Projects span two to three sessions. Introduce Git here, on something small.

**Term 3 — An open project.** Learners choose. They will pick something too ambitious; let them, then help them scope it down in week two rather than week eight. End with a showcase — an audience does more for quality than any amount of marking.

Three principles that matter more than the content:

- **Finish things.** A completed simple project beats an abandoned impressive one, every time. This is the single biggest predictor of whether a learner continues.
- **Pairs, not solos.** Two per kit produces discussion, debugging and explanation. One per kit produces silent instruction-following.
- **Let the failures happen.** A circuit that doesn't work is the lesson. Rescuing too early removes the entire learning opportunity — and learners can tell when they've been rescued.
