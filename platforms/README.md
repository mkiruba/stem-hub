## Choosing, briefly

**Never touched hardware?** micro:bit. The onboard sensors mean your first ten projects need nothing else, which removes wiring errors from the equation entirely.

**Comfortable with code, want to build things?** Pico 2 W. Five to seven pounds for a dual-core WiFi microcontroller with excellent documentation is unmatched.

**Want cameras, vision, AI, or a web server?** Raspberry Pi. It's the only option here that runs Linux.

**Following a specific tutorial?** Use whatever it uses. Fighting a substitution while also learning is two problems at once.

**Want to make wearables or soft circuits?** Adafruit — Circuit Playground and the Flora range, plus Becky Stern's guides.

**No budget yet?** [Wokwi](https://wokwi.com/) and [Tinkercad Circuits](https://www.tinkercad.com/circuits) simulate most of this for free in a browser.

## A common mistake

Picking the platform before picking the project. A learner who wants to make a light-up jacket and gets handed a Raspberry Pi will lose interest, and a learner who wants to do face recognition on a micro:bit will hit a wall.

Start from [themes](../themes/README.md) — what do they actually want to make? — and let that choose the board.
